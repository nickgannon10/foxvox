In neural-network training, what does learning-rate annealing mean?	Gradually reducing the optimizer&#x27;s learning rate during training, often near the end. Later updates become smaller, allowing more precise adjustment of the weights.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11029v1">https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11029v1</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::optimization recall_id_annealing-definition
Why can reducing the learning rate near the end of training improve validation loss?	Smaller updates reduce the fluctuations caused by a large step size and noisy gradients. The model can settle more precisely; reducing the rate too early can slow useful learning.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11029v1">https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11029v1</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::optimization recall_id_annealing-purpose

No. A final-loss fit can be conditional on a chosen training schedule without including that schedule as an explicit variable. In reinforcement learning, what are the behavior policy and the target policy?	The behavior policy generates the actions in the training data. The target policy is the policy whose performance or value we are learning about or trying to improve. They can differ.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/user/algorithms.html">https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/user/algorithms.html</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl recall_id_behavior-target-policy
What makes an RL learning method on-policy?	It learns about the policy used to collect its data. In policy optimization, updates use fresh trajectories from the current or just-previous policy rather than arbitrary old experience.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/user/algorithms.html">https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/user/algorithms.html</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl recall_id_on-policy
What makes an RL learning method off-policy?	It can learn about a target policy from actions generated by a different behavior policy. This enables reuse of older experience, subject to the algorithm&#x27;s assumptions and data coverage.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/user/algorithms.html">https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/user/algorithms.html</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl recall_id_off-policy
Which are usually classified as on-policy versus off-policy: PPO, DDPG, and SAC?	PPO is usually classified as on-policy. DDPG and SAC are off-policy and can train from replay buffers.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/user/algorithms.html">https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/user/algorithms.html</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl recall_id_algorithm-examples
Why does PPO's reuse of a recent rollout for several optimization epochs not make it an arbitrary replay-buffer method?	It reuses a batch collected by a recent policy, tracks the old action probabilities, and discourages large changes through its objective. It then gathers fresh rollouts. This differs from freely training on old replay data.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347">https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl recall_id_ppo-reuse
Does PPO clipping guarantee that the new-to-old action-probability ratio stays inside the clipping interval?	No. Clipping changes the surrogate objective and removes some incentives for large changes. It does not enforce a hard bound on the actual probability ratio or on KL divergence.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/algorithms/ppo.html">https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/algorithms/ppo.html</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl recall_id_ppo-clipping-not-bound
What does non-stationarity mean in a learning problem?	The distribution or task being learned changes over time. For example, training moves from broad language data to narrow domain-specific instructions, or from one task to another.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16789">https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16789</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::continual_learning recall_id_nonstationarity
What is catastrophic forgetting during sequential fine-tuning?	Performance on previously learned tasks or capabilities deteriorates as the model learns new ones. Improvement on the newest task alone does not establish that earlier abilities were retained.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08747">https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08747</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::continual_learning recall_id_forgetting-definition
How can a shift to a new SFT dataset cause forgetting of earlier capabilities?	The new objective updates shared parameters to fit the new data. Those changes can damage parameters useful for old tasks, while missing old examples provide no corrective training signal. Distribution shift creates this risk; forgetting is not inevitable.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796">https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::continual_learning recall_id_sft-forgetting-mechanism
Can a fixed, shuffled SFT dataset still lead to forgetting?	Yes. The dataset may be stationary within that phase, yet differ from pretraining or an earlier fine-tuning phase. The shift between phases can produce interference. Random minibatch variation alone is not non-stationarity.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16789">https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16789</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::continual_learning recall_id_fixed-sft-nonstationarity
Name two ways to reduce catastrophic forgetting, and the principle behind each.	Replay or mix earlier examples to retain a training signal for old tasks. Regularize important parameters to discourage changes that damage earlier solutions. Neither guarantees retention; evaluate old tasks as well as the new one.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796">https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::continual_learning recall_id_forgetting-mitigation
What is the key distinction between online and offline RL?	Online RL collects additional environment experience during learning. Offline RL trains from previously collected data without additional environment interaction during that training phase.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01643">https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01643</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl recall_id_online-offline
Can RL be online and off-policy at the same time? Give an example.	Yes. A SAC agent can keep collecting new experience while learning from a replay buffer containing older policies&#x27; data. Online/offline concerns data collection; on/off-policy concerns the relationship between data-generating and target policies.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01643">https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01643</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl recall_id_online-offpolicy
Why is applying an ordinary off-policy algorithm to a fixed offline dataset often difficult?	The learned policy may choose actions poorly covered by the dataset and exploit inaccurate value estimates. Without fresh interaction, it cannot directly collect evidence to correct those errors.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01643">https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01643</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl recall_id_offline-danger
What does synchronous training mean in distributed RL?	Workers coordinate at barriers before an update or the next phase. In a common rollout-then-update design, a batch is collected with fixed policy weights, the learner updates, and the new weights are broadcast. Slow workers can delay progress.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/algorithms/ppo.html">https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/algorithms/ppo.html</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl_systems recall_id_sync-rl
What does asynchronous training mean in distributed RL?	Actors and learners, or multiple learning workers, progress without waiting at a global barrier for every update. This can improve utilization but may produce stale trajectories or gradients.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01783">https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01783</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl_systems recall_id_async-rl
How can asynchronous actor-learner training create off-policy data?	An actor may generate a trajectory using old policy weights while the learner has already updated. The behavior policy and learner&#x27;s target policy then differ: this is policy lag.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01561">https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01561</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl_systems recall_id_policy-lag
What problem does V-trace address in IMPALA?	It corrects for the mismatch between the policy that generated actor trajectories and the learner&#x27;s policy, enabling stable learning with decoupled acting and learning.<p><small>Sources: <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01561">https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01561</a></small></p>	General	recall recall_batch_20260912 recall::ml recall::rl_systems recall_id_impala-vtrace
What three different questions do online/offline, on/off-policy, and sync/async answer?	Online/offline: is new environment data collected? On/off-policy: how does the behavior policy relate to the policy being learned? Sync/async: when do workers wait and coordinate?
