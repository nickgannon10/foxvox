<b>Algebra · a rational inequality</b><br><br>Solve over the reals:<br>\[\frac{x+1}{x-2}\ge 2.\] Remember to account for the denominator.	\[\boxed{x\in(2,5]}\]<p>Move everything to one side: \(\frac{x+1}{x-2}-2=\frac{5-x}{x-2}\ge0\). The critical points are \(2\) (undefined) and \(5\) (zero). The expression is negative for \(x&lt;2\), positive for \(2&lt;x&lt;5\), and negative for \(x&gt;5\). Include \(5\), exclude \(2\).</p>	General
<b>Algebra · polynomial remainders</b><br><br>Find the remainder when \(x^4-3x^2+2x+5\) is divided by \(x^2-x-1\), without performing long division.	\[\boxed{2x+4}\]<p>Modulo \(x^2-x-1\), we have \(x^2\equiv x+1\). Thus \(x^3\equiv2x+1\) and \(x^4\equiv3x+2\). Substitute: \((3x+2)-3(x+1)+2x+5=2x+4\). Its degree is less than \(2\), so this is the remainder.</p>	General
<b>Algebra · a recurrence</b><br><br>Find a closed form for \(a_n\), where \(a_0=1\), \(a_1=3\), and<br>\[a_{n+2}=3a_{n+1}-2a_n\qquad(n\ge0).\]	\[\boxed{a_n=2^{n+1}-1}\]<p>The characteristic equation is \(r^2-3r+2=(r-1)(r-2)=0\). Therefore \(a_n=A+B2^n\). The initial conditions give \(A+B=1\) and \(A+2B=3\), so \(B=2\), \(A=-1\).</p>	General
<b>Calculus · an exact integral</b><br><br>Evaluate:<br>\[\int_0^1 x\ln(1+x)\,dx.\]	\[\boxed{\frac14}\]<p>Integrate by parts with \(u=\ln(1+x)\), \(dv=x\,dx\): \(I=\frac12\ln2-\frac12\int_0^1\frac{x^2}{1+x}\,dx\). Use \(\frac{x^2}{1+x}=x-1+\frac1{1+x}\). The remaining integral is \(\ln2-\frac12\), so \(I=\frac14\).</p>	General
<b>Calculus · the first surviving term</b><br><br>Find the two-sided limit:<br>\[\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\ln(1+x)-x+x^2/2}{x^3}.\]	\[\boxed{\frac13}\]<p>Near \(0\), \(\ln(1+x)=x-\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{x^3}{3}+O(x^4)\). The linear and quadratic terms cancel, leaving \(\frac{x^3}{3}+O(x^4)\). Divide by \(x^3\). Alternatively, applying l'Hôpital's rule three times gives \(2/6\).</p>	General
<b>Calculus · constrained optimization</b><br><br>A <b>closed</b> right circular cylinder has volume \(54\pi\). What radius \(r\) and height \(h\) minimize its total surface area?	\[\boxed{r=3,\quad h=6,\quad S_{\min}=54\pi}\]<p>The volume constraint gives \(h=54/r^2\), with \(r&gt;0\). Thus \(S(r)=2\pi r^2+108\pi/r\), and \(S'(r)=4\pi r-108\pi/r^2\). Setting it to zero gives \(r^3=27\). Also \(S''(r)=4\pi+216\pi/r^3&gt;0\), so this is the unique minimum. The optimal height equals the diameter.</p>	General
<b>Probability · update, then predict</b><br><br>Choose one of two urns with equal probability. Urn A has 3 red balls and 1 blue; urn B has 1 red and 3 blue. Draw <b>with replacement</b> from that same urn. The first two draws are red. What is the probability that the third is red?	\[\boxed{\frac7{10}}\]<p>Bayes' rule gives \(P(A\mid RR)=\frac{(1/2)(3/4)^2}{(1/2)(3/4)^2+(1/2)(1/4)^2}=9/10\). Average the next-draw probability over the updated urn probabilities: \((9/10)(3/4)+(1/10)(1/4)=7/10\). Draws are independent conditional on the urn, not when the urn is unknown.</p>	General
<b>Probability · a conditional area</b><br><br>Let \(X,Y\) be independent and uniform on \([0,1]\). Find:<br>\[P\!\left(\max(X,Y)&gt;\frac34\;\middle|\;X+Y&gt;1\right).\]	\[\boxed{\frac34}\]<p>The condition \(X+Y&gt;1\) occupies half the unit square, area \(1/2\). Within it, the excluded region \(X\le3/4\), \(Y\le3/4\) is a right triangle with legs \(1/2\), area \(1/8\). The desired conditional probability is \(\frac{1/2-1/8}{1/2}=3/4\). Boundaries have probability zero.</p>	General
<b>Probability · a waiting time</b><br><br>Roll a fair six-sided die repeatedly. Starting before any rolls, what is the expected number of rolls until you get <b>two consecutive sixes</b>?	\[\boxed{42\text{ rolls}}\]<p>Let \(E_0\) be the expected remaining rolls with no current six-streak, and \(E_1\) after one six. First-step equations give \(E_0=1+\frac56E_0+\frac16E_1\) and \(E_1=1+\frac56E_0\); a six from state 1 finishes. Hence \(E_0=6+E_1=7+\frac56E_0\), so \(E_0=42\).</p>	General
<b>Linear algebra · orthogonal projection</b><br><br>Project \(b=(1,2,3)^T\) orthogonally onto the plane \(x+y+z=0\). Also find the distance from \(b\) to the plane.	\[\boxed{\operatorname{proj}(b)=(-1,0,1)^T,\qquad d=2\sqrt3}\]<p>A normal is \(n=(1,1,1)^T\). The normal component is \(\frac{b\cdot n}{n\cdot n}n=\frac63n=(2,2,2)^T\). Subtract it from \(b\) for the projection. The distance is the norm of that normal component: \(\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3\).</p>	General
<b>Linear algebra · powers without repeated multiplication</b><br><br>For an integer \(n\ge0\), find a closed form for:<br>\[\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}^{n}.\]	\[\boxed{\frac12\begin{pmatrix}3^n+1&3^n-1\\3^n-1&3^n+1\end{pmatrix}}\]<p>The vectors \((1,1)^T\) and \((1,-1)^T\) are orthogonal eigenvectors, with eigenvalues \(3\) and \(1\). Their orthogonal projectors are \(P_+=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\) and \(P_-=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\). Therefore \(A^n=3^nP_++P_-\), including \(n=0\).</p>	General
<b>Linear algebra · least squares</b><br><br>Fit a line \(y=a+bx\) by ordinary least squares to the points \((0,1)\), \((1,2)\), and \((2,2)\). Find \(a\), \(b\), and the sum of squared residuals.	\[\boxed{a=\frac76,\quad b=\frac12,\quad \mathrm{SSE}=\frac16}\]<p>Here \(\bar x=1\) and \(\bar y=5/3\). The slope is \(b=\frac{\sum(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2}=1/2\); then \(a=\bar y-b\bar x=7/6\). The residuals \(y_i-\hat y_i\) are \((-1/6,1/3,-1/6)\), whose squared sum is \(1/6\).</p>	General
